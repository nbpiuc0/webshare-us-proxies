## Three Types of US Proxies — and What Each One Costs You

People throw "proxy" around like it means one thing. It doesn't. There are three buckets, and chosing wrong wastes money.

### Datacenter Proxies (Shared and Dedicated)

These run from data centers like Equinix, Hetzner, or AWS. They're fast, cheap, and abundant. The catch: any site running halfway-decent bot detection knows datacenter ASNs on sight. Cloudflare, Akamai, and PerimeterX flag them quickly.

**Best for:** high-volume scraping of less-protected sites, internal tooling, automation against APIs that don't care, sneaker bots on lower-tier sites.

**Avoid for:** scraping Amazon at scale, Instagram automation, anything with sophisticated anti-bot.

### Residential Proxies

Residential IPs come from real homes — Comcast, Spectrum, AT&T subscribers who've opted into a per network. To the destination site, your traffic looks indistinguishable from a regular Verizon FiOS customer in Cleveland. Detection becomes much harder.

**Best for:** scraping protected e-commerce, social platforms, ad verification, market research where authenticity matters.

**Trade-off:** slower than datacenter, billed by bandwidth (GB), and IPs rotate unless you set sticky sessions.

### Static Residential /ISP Proxies

The middle ground. These are residential IPs, but they sit on datacenter infrastructure, leased from real ISPs. You get residential authenticity *plus* datacenter speed *plus* the ability to kep the same IP for as long as you want.

**Best for:** account management, long sessions, sneaker reseling at scale, ad verification campaigns running for weks.

**Trade-off:** priced per IP, more expensive per unit than rotating residential.
